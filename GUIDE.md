# Web Portal Quality Guide: decision guide

Connect a portal journey to the evidence its users need.

**For:** Digital product owners, portal owners and QA leads discussing customer, employee or partner experiences.

**Decision in focus:** Which part of the user journey has an unresolved quality or accessibility question?

## Define a meaningful user journey

A portal outcome can involve finding information, requesting a service, completing a transaction or checking status. Begin with the user and outcome, rather than a list of screens. The examples are illustrative categories, not observations about any existing customer product.

**Question to discuss:** What should a person be able to complete, and how will the owner recognise success?

## Discuss identity, errors and external handovers

Sign-in, role-dependent visibility, interrupted tasks and external services can change the experience. Ask which of these matter to the proposed change and who owns the evidence. Do not submit real accounts, customer details, portal URLs with tokens or screenshots to public issues.

**Question to discuss:** Where can the journey stop or cross into another accountable system?

## Treat accessibility as an evaluation question

W3C WAI provides guidance on evaluating web accessibility. Automated tools can help but cannot alone establish conformance. Discuss user needs, scope and human evaluation; this site supplies no accessibility audit or statement of conformity.

**Question to discuss:** Which user needs or interaction modes are not explained by current evidence?

## Connect experience and business acceptance

Browser performance, device context and cross-platform responses can affect whether a person completes the task. Core Web Vitals are one evidence source, not a complete definition of quality. A discovery brief should identify the unresolved journey question and its owner.

**Question to discuss:** Which uncertainty most affects the user outcome or the next release decision?
